Cash Equity

Buying an actual share

You hand over cash today and get a small, permanent slice of a company in return — no expiry date, no contract to unwind later.

1

What it actually is

Cash equity just means buying an actual share of a company for cash, settled almost immediately — as opposed to a derivative that only references the share's price.

You own it outright: no expiry, no counterparty who owes you anything once the trade settles. It's the plainest trade in finance.

2

Why someone would do this

You buy a share because you think the company will be worth more later, because it pays dividends, or because you want a vote at the AGM.

There's no clever structuring here — it's the most direct way to own a piece of a business, which is exactly why it's most people's first trade.

3

Who's actually doing this

Pretty much everyone with a pension or a brokerage account owns cash equity somewhere. Index funds hold it in bulk to track a market; active managers pick individual names; companies buy back their own stock.

Every derivative later in this course — the option, the swap — exists because this trade did first.

Settlement: T+1 Ownership: outright, no expiry Rights: dividends + vote The building block for every equity derivative
