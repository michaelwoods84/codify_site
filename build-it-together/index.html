Who this is for

You don't need to know the answers.

Professional programmers spend most of their day stuck. These projects are designed for parents with no coding experience: you read the steps, your child drives, and you get unstuck together.

You'll get a lot from this if you…

  • Want to do something hands-on with your child, not just read about it
  • Have never coded and feel a bit nervous about it
  • Have a child curious about games, gadgets or AI
  • Can find 30–60 minutes at a weekend

This probably isn't for you if…

  • Your child already codes confidently on their own
  • You want a formal programming qualification
  • You don’t have access to a laptop or desktop computer

Common questions

FAQ

Do I need any coding experience?

None. The projects are written for parents who have never coded. You read the instructions; your child does the building.

What equipment do we need?

A laptop or desktop computer with a web browser. Every tool used is free. A BBC micro:bit is optional for the final project — there’s a free on-screen simulator.

What ages is it for?

Roughly 7–14. Younger children will need more help with reading the steps; older children can work more independently and try the extension ideas.

How does it relate to the parents’ guide?

“What Is My Child Actually Learning?” explains what children learn about coding, data and AI. This course lets you do it together — each project links back to a module in the guide.
